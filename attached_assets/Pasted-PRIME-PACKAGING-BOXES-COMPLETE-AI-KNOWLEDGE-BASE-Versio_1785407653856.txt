--- PRINTING TECHNOLOGIES ---

CMYK FULL COLOR PRINTING:
CMYK stands for Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Black â€” the four ink colors used in standard full color printing. CMYK printing can reproduce virtually any color and is suitable for photographs, complex gradients, and multi-color designs. It is the most common printing method for packaging and offers excellent color vibrancy and consistency across all units in an order.

PANTONE SPOT COLOR MATCHING:
Pantone is a standardized color matching system used worldwide by designers and printers. If your brand has official Pantone color codes, we can match them precisely in printing. This is important for brands that have strict brand color guidelines and need absolute consistency across all printed materials including packaging. Share your Pantone color numbers and we will match them exactly.

DIGITAL PRINTING:
Digital printing transfers ink directly from a digital file to the packaging material without the need for printing plates. It is ideal for shorter print runs (under 500 units typically), fast turnaround times, and orders that require variable data printing or frequent design changes. Color quality is excellent for digital printing.

OFFSET LITHOGRAPHY PRINTING:
Offset printing uses printing plates and transfers ink via rollers to the printing surface. It produces the highest possible color consistency across very large print runs and is the most cost-effective method for large quantity orders (typically 1000 units and above). Color accuracy and consistency are exceptional with offset printing.

FLEXOGRAPHIC PRINTING:
Flexographic printing (flexo) is commonly used for printing on corrugated cardboard because of the uneven surface of corrugated board. It uses flexible rubber printing plates and fast-drying inks. Flexo can print in multiple colors and is cost-effective for large volume corrugated box orders.

WHITE INK PRINTING:
White ink printing is used when printing on kraft paper, dark colored materials, or transparent materials where standard inks would not show up clearly. White ink creates a bright white base layer that allows other colors to be printed on top with full vibrancy even on dark or non-white materials.

--- SURFACE FINISHES AND COATINGS ---

GLOSS LAMINATION:
A thin, glossy film is laminated onto the printed surface of the box. Gloss lamination creates a shiny, vibrant finish that makes colors appear more vivid and saturated. It is fingerprint resistant and provides excellent protection against scuffing. Gloss lamination is the most popular finish for retail packaging and gives boxes a polished, professional appearance.

MATTE LAMINATION:
A thin, non-glossy film is laminated onto the printed surface. Matte lamination creates a sophisticated, understated finish with reduced light reflection. It gives packaging a premium, editorial feel and is particularly popular for luxury, beauty, and high-end retail products. Text and design elements are very readable against a matte surface.

SOFT TOUCH LAMINATION:
Also called velvet lamination, soft touch is a specialty lamination film that creates a velvety, tactile surface that feels luxuriously smooth and soft to the touch. Soft touch lamination elevates the perceived premium quality of packaging significantly and creates a memorable physical experience when customers handle the product. It is particularly popular for luxury cosmetics, premium food brands, and high-end consumer products.

SPOT UV COATING:
Spot UV is a glossy, clear coating applied to specific areas of the packaging surface (rather than the entire surface) to create contrast between glossy highlighted areas and the surrounding matte or uncoated surface. Spot UV is often used to highlight logos, product names, or design elements, creating a dramatic visual effect that catches the light and draws attention.

AQUEOUS COATING:
A clear, water-based protective coating applied over printing to protect the surface from scratching, scuffing, and moisture. Aqueous coating is available in gloss and matte finishes and is a cost-effective way to protect packaging without the higher cost of lamination.

--- SPECIAL EFFECTS AND EMBELLISHMENTS ---

GOLD FOIL STAMPING:
A metallic gold foil is applied to specific areas of the packaging using a heated die and pressure. Gold foil creates a luxurious, premium appearance that immediately communicates quality and exclusivity. It is commonly used for logos, brand names, and decorative borders on premium packaging. Gold foil is one of the most popular embellishments for luxury brands.

SILVER FOIL STAMPING:
Similar to gold foil but using metallic silver foil. Silver foil creates a sleek, modern, premium effect. It is popular for technology brands, beauty brands, and any packaging that needs a contemporary luxury feel. Silver foil works beautifully on both dark and light backgrounds.

HOLOGRAPHIC FOIL:
Holographic foil creates a multi-colored, rainbow shimmer effect that shifts and changes as the viewing angle changes. Holographic foil is extremely eye-catching and is used for premium packaging, special edition products, collectibles, and any packaging designed to attract maximum attention on retail shelves.

EMBOSSING:
Embossing is a process that presses a design, logo, or pattern into the packaging material from the back, creating a raised three-dimensional effect on the front surface. The raised area can be felt with the fingers and creates a premium tactile experience. Embossing is commonly used for logos, brand names, and decorative patterns on luxury packaging.

DEBOSSING:
Debossing is the opposite of embossing â€” a design, logo, or pattern is pressed into the packaging material from the front, creating a recessed three-dimensional impression in the surface. Debossing creates a sophisticated, subtle effect and is commonly used for monograms, logos, and decorative elements on luxury packaging.

CUSTOM DIE-CUTTING:
Die-cutting uses a custom steel blade (die) to cut packaging materials into any shape. It allows for the creation of unique box shapes, decorative cutouts, window openings, handles, tabs, and any other structural feature that cannot be achieved with straight cuts alone. Die-cutting is what allows us to create any custom box shape the customer can imagine.

PERFORATIONS:
Perforated lines are small cuts that allow packaging to be torn open easily along a defined line. Perforations are used for easy-open packaging, promotional tear-off sections, coupons, and packaging that needs to be opened without scissors or tools.

EMBOSSED FOIL COMBINATIONS:
Embossing and foil stamping can be combined in a single process for maximum premium effect. The result is a raised area with a metallic foil finish â€” an extremely luxurious combination popular for high-end jewelry boxes, luxury cosmetics, and premium spirits packaging.

--- PAPERBOARD MATERIALS ---

SOLID BLEACHED SULFATE (SBS):
SBS board is the premium white paperboard used for high-quality retail packaging and folding cartons. It has a pure white, smooth surface on one or both sides that accepts printing with exceptional color vibrancy and detail. SBS is the material of choice for cosmetics, food packaging, pharmaceuticals, and other premium retail applications. Available in weights from 12pt to 24pt.

COATED UNBLEACHED KRAFT (CUK):
CUK is a natural kraft board with a white coated surface on one side and natural brown on the other. It provides the eco-friendly aesthetic of kraft paper combined with the printability of a white coated surface. Popular for food packaging and products that want to communicate natural and sustainable values.

KRAFT PAPERBOARD:
Natural unbleached kraft board with a distinctive brown color. Kraft packaging communicates natural, organic, artisan, and eco-friendly values. It prints beautifully with appropriate ink colors (particularly black, white, and earth tones) and is popular for food brands, organic products, artisan goods, and sustainable brands.

RECYCLED BOARD:
Paperboard made from post-consumer recycled paper content. Recycled board is the most environmentally responsible option and is available in a range of surface qualities. It meets the growing demand for packaging with genuine recycled content.

RIGID CHIPBOARD:
Thick, dense board used for rigid setup boxes. Chipboard does not fold and maintains its shape permanently, making it ideal for luxury boxes. Available in thicknesses from 1mm to 3mm or more depending on requirements.

--- CORRUGATED MATERIALS ---

E-FLUTE CORRUGATED (1.5mm):
The thinnest corrugated board profile. E-flute has a very smooth surface that accepts printing almost as well as paperboard. It provides light cushioning and rigidity while maintaining a slim profile. Ideal for retail-ready corrugated boxes and e-commerce mailers that need to balance printability with protection.

B-FLUTE CORRUGATED (3mm):
Medium thickness corrugated with good crush resistance. B-flute is popular for POS displays, pizza boxes, glass packaging, and applications where moderate protection and good printability are both needed.

C-FLUTE CORRUGATED (4mm):
The most common corrugated board profile, used for the majority of standard shipping boxes. C-flute offers a good balance of strength, cushioning, and printability. It is the standard for most product shipping applications.

BC DOUBLE WALL CORRUGATED (7mm):
Combining B-flute and C-flute in a double wall construction for maximum strength and protection. BC double wall is used for heavy products, long-distance shipping, industrial packaging, and anything that requires the highest level of protection.

ECT (EDGE CRUSH TEST) RATED BOARD:
Corrugated board rated by its edge crush strength rather than wall thickness. ECT rated board is used when specific structural performance requirements must be met, such as for palletized stacking or carrier-specified requirements.

--- SPECIALTY MATERIALS ---

FOOD-SAFE MATERIALS:
FDA-compliant materials specifically manufactured for direct food contact applications. These materials do not contain harmful chemicals that could migrate into food products. Required for all food contact packaging.

MOISTURE RESISTANT COATINGS:
Coatings applied to paperboard or corrugated to resist moisture penetration. Important for food packaging, refrigerated product packaging, and packaging used in humid environments.

GREASE RESISTANT COATINGS:
Specialized coatings that prevent grease and oils from penetrating packaging material. Essential for pizza boxes, fried food packaging, bakery packaging, and any food packaging where grease contact is likely.

ANTI-STATIC MATERIALS:
Packaging materials that dissipate static electricity, protecting sensitive electronic components from electrostatic discharge. Used for electronics and electronic component packaging.

IMPORTANT: We do not have fixed published prices because every order is custom-made to unique specifications. Pricing is always calculated individually based on the specific requirements of each order.

FACTORS THAT DETERMINE PRICE:

1. BOX STYLE AND COMPLEXITY:
Simple tuck-top carton boxes are generally the most affordable. Rigid boxes, boxes with custom structural features, and complex die-cut shapes cost more due to the additional manufacturing complexity.

2. BOX DIMENSIONS:
Larger boxes require more material and therefore cost more per unit than smaller boxes of the same style.

3. ORDER QUANTITY:
This is the most significant pricing factor. The more boxes ordered, the lower the per-unit cost. This is because setup costs (plate-making, die creation, machine setup) are amortized across more units. Ordering 1000 boxes instead of 100 boxes can reduce the per-unit price by 30-60% in many cases.

4. MATERIAL SELECTION:
Premium materials like rigid chipboard, soft touch lamination, and specialty boards cost more than standard corrugated or SBS board.

5. PRINTING COLORS AND COVERAGE:
Full coverage printing with multiple colors costs more than single color or limited coverage printing. However, for offset printing at larger quantities, color count has less impact on price.

6. FINISHING OPTIONS:
Each premium finishing option (foil stamping, embossing, soft touch lamination, spot UV) adds cost but also significantly increases perceived value.

7. TURNAROUND TIME:
Rush production requests may incur additional fees depending on how much faster than standard production the customer requires.

8. CUSTOM FEATURES:
Windows, magnetic closures, ribbon pulls, foam inserts, and other custom features each add to the cost.

GENERAL VOLUME PRICING GUIDANCE:
- 100 units: base pricing, highest per-unit cost
- 250 units: approximately 15-25% lower per unit than 100
- 500 units: approximately 25-35% lower per unit than 100
- 1000 units: approximately 35-50% lower per unit than 100
- 2500 units: approximately 50-60% lower per unit than 100
- 5000 units: maximum bulk discount, lowest per-unit pricing

--- TUCK TOP AUTO BOTTOM BOX (TTAB) ---
One of the most common folding carton styles. The top of the box has tuck flaps that fold in to close the box. The bottom automatically locks into place when the box is assembled, requiring no glue or tape at the bottom. This style is fast to assemble on a production line and provides a reliable secure bottom. It is ideal for retail products, cosmetics, food items, and general consumer products.

Advantages: Fast assembly, secure bottom, cost-effective

--- REVERSE TUCK END BOX (RTE) ---
Similar to tuck top auto bottom but both the top and bottom have tuck flaps that fold in from opposite sides. The top tuck folds from the front and the bottom tuck folds from the back (or vice versa). This creates a clean look on all sides. It is one of the most widely used folding carton styles in consumer goods packaging.

Best uses: Pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, food items, electronics accessories
Assembly: Both ends tuck closed from opposite sides
Advantages: Neat appearance on all panels, very common and familiar style

--- STRAIGHT TUCK END BOX (STE) ---
Both the top and bottom tuck flaps fold in from the same side. This creates a specific appearance that works well for certain display orientations. It is slightly less common than reverse tuck but preferred for certain applications.

Best uses: Retail products where one side faces the consumer
Assembly: Both ends tuck from the same direction
Advantages: Clean front-facing appearance

--- SNAP LOCK BOTTOM BOX ---
Also known as a 1-2-3 bottom or auto lock bottom, this style has a bottom that locks together by folding flaps in a specific sequence without glue. It is stronger than a standard tuck bottom and assembles quickly. Often used for heavier products.

Best uses: Heavier retail products, gift sets, food products requiring stronger bottoms
Assembly: Bottom panels lock together in sequence
Advantages: Strong bottom, no glue required, fast assembly

--- SEAL END BOX ---
Both the top and bottom have flaps that are glued shut at the factory. Seal end boxes arrive pre-glued and only need to be filled from one end. They are very strong and are commonly used for soap bars, candles, and other products where a fully sealed appearance is important.

Best uses: Soap bars, candles, food items, products needing fully sealed packaging

Advantages: Very strong, fully sealed appearance, no assembly of bottom required

--- GABLE TOP BOX ---
A distinctive box style with a peaked, triangular roof at the top that forms a carrying handle. Gable top boxes are immediately recognizable and are commonly used for food and beverage products, takeaway food containers, and gift packaging. They stand out on shelves and are very memorable.

TYPOGRAPHY GUIDELINES FOR PACKAGING:
- Brand name should be the largest and most prominent text element
- Product name or type should be clearly readable at typical viewing distances
- Supporting information (tagline, key benefits) should be secondary in size
- Legal and regulatory text can be small but must be legible
- Limit the number of different fonts to 2-3 maximum for a clean look
- Ensure text has sufficient contrast against the background color
- Avoid placing text over complex background images where readability suffers

DESIGN ELEMENTS THAT SELL:
- Clear product name visible from a distance
- Key benefit or differentiator stated prominently
- High quality product photography or illustration where appropriate
- Brand logo in a consistent and prominent position
- Color scheme consistent with brand identity
- Contact information and website
- Any certifications or quality marks relevant to the product
- Recycling information if appropriate

WHAT TO AVOID IN PACKAGING DESIGN:
- Too much text that overwhelms the viewer
- Too many competing design elements
- Colors that do not work together or clash
- Low-resolution images that print blurry
- Insufficient contrast between text and background
- Ignoring required regulatory information
- Design that does not match the product quality level

DIELINE EXPLAINED:
A dieline is the flat technical template of the box showing all fold lines, cut lines, and glue areas. It is the technical blueprint of the box structure. When our design team creates your packaging design, they apply your artwork to the dieline to show exactly how every panel will look when the box is assembled. This ensures there are no surprises when the physical box is produced.

BLEED AND SAFE ZONE:
- Bleed: The area of the design that extends beyond the cut line (typically 3mm or 1/8 inch). Bleed ensures no white edges appear if there is slight movement during cutting.
- Safe Zone: The area inside the cut line where all important content (text, logos) should stay. Typically 3-5mm inside the cut line. Content in the safe zone is guaranteed not to be cut off.

Our design team handles bleed and safe zone setup automatically when preparing your print files.

SECTION 31 â€” PACKAGING CARE AND STORAGE GUIDE
================================================================

HOW TO STORE YOUR CUSTOM BOXES:
Proper storage of packaging inventory ensures your boxes remain in perfect condition until use.

IDEAL STORAGE CONDITIONS:
- Temperature: Room temperature, 60-80Â°F (15-27Â°C)
- Humidity: 40-60% relative humidity
- Avoid: Direct sunlight (can fade colors and warp board)
- Avoid: High humidity (can cause board to absorb moisture and warp)
- Avoid: Extreme cold (can make board brittle)
- Avoid: Stacking too high (can crush bottom boxes)

STORAGE TIPS:
- Store boxes flat in their shipping master cartons until ready to use
- Keep boxes on shelving or pallets off the ground to prevent moisture absorption
- Do not store near HVAC vents, windows, or exterior walls
- First in, first out â€” use oldest inventory first
- Inspect a sample of boxes before use if stored for more than 6 months

SHELF LIFE OF CUSTOM PACKAGING:
Properly stored custom packaging typically maintains its quality for 12-24 months. Packaging stored beyond this period should be inspected before use.

HANDLING TIPS:
- Handle boxes with clean, dry hands or cotton gloves for premium finishes
- Soft touch lamination is particularly susceptible to fingerprints and marks
- Foil-stamped boxes should be handled carefully to prevent scratching
- Avoid dragging boxes across rough surfaces

CHOOSING THE RIGHT STRENGTH FOR YOUR BOX:

LIGHT DUTY (Folding Carton / SBS Board):
Best for: Retail products, cosmetics, food cartons, supplements
Product weight: Under 2 lbs (1 kg)
Shipping suitability: Retail shelf, hand delivery, lightweight shipping
Examples: Lipstick box, vitamin carton, soap box, candy box

MEDIUM DUTY (E-Flute Corrugated):
Best for: E-commerce products, mailer boxes, light retail shipping
Product weight: 2-10 lbs (1-4.5 kg)
Shipping suitability: Standard postal and courier shipping
Examples: Clothing mailer, small electronics, book sets, candle sets

STANDARD DUTY (B or C-Flute Corrugated):
Best for: General product shipping, heavier retail items
Product weight: 10-30 lbs (4.5-13.5 kg)
Shipping suitability: Standard and heavy shipping
Examples: Multi-product bundles, home goods, heavier electronics

HEAVY DUTY (Double Wall BC Corrugated):
Best for: Heavy products, industrial items, palletized shipping
Product weight: 30-80 lbs (13.5-36 kg)
Shipping suitability: Freight and heavy shipping
Examples: Machine parts, bulk food, appliances

VERY HEAVY DUTY (Triple Wall Corrugated):
Best for: Very heavy industrial applications
Product weight: 80+ lbs
Shipping suitability: Freight, specialized shipping

HOW TO REDUCE YOUR PACKAGING COST WITHOUT SACRIFICING QUALITY:

TIP 1 â€” ORDER MORE UNITS:
The single biggest factor in per-unit cost is quantity. Even increasing from 500 to 1000 units can reduce per-unit cost by 20-30%.

TIP 2 â€” SIMPLIFY YOUR DESIGN:
Full coverage printing over 4 colors on every panel is more expensive than a focused design. A clean, minimalist design can look more premium while costing less.

TIP 3 â€” CHOOSE STANDARD FINISHES:
Matte or gloss lamination (standard) costs significantly less than soft touch, foil, or embossing. A well-designed box with standard finishing can look excellent.

TIP 4 â€” PLAN AHEAD â€” AVOID RUSH FEES:
Rush production adds 20-40% to your cost. Ordering with adequate lead time (3-4 weeks) avoids rush fees entirely.

TIP 5 â€” OPTIMIZE BOX SIZE:
Unnecessarily large boxes waste material and increase cost. The tightest practical fit for your product gives the best cost.

TIP 6 â€” STANDARDIZE YOUR BOX SIZES:
If you sell multiple products, designing them all to fit in one or two standard box sizes can reduce die costs and allow larger combined quantity orders.

TIP 7 â€” USE OUR FREE DESIGN SERVICE:
Our design service is free. Using it instead of an external designer saves design fees.

TIP 8 â€” REORDER STRATEGICALLY:
Plan reorders before you run out. Rush reorders cost more. Order enough to last 3-6 months at a time.
